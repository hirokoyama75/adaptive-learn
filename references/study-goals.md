# Study goals, not ability levels

Read this for a goal switch, in-depth learning, or uncertain exam intent. Retain current evidence and support needs across switches. Do not make the learner choose a mode on every turn.

| Goal | Start here | Keep checking |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cramming | Identify supplied scope, remaining effort if known, and consequential bottlenecks. Start a representative high-value task. | What can still be practised independently? What remains uncovered/untested? Is the weighting known or just assumed? |
| Learning for class | Use supplied course goals and current material; connect explanation to expected assignments/assessment. | Can the learner apply the idea, and retrieve it later without the current explanation? What does a practice paper fail to cover? |
| Learning in depth | Start with an observable question or capability; connect concrete explanation to a usable model. | Can the learner explain, apply, distinguish, and identify limits—not merely repeat a polished analogy? |

An exam in three months defaults to class-aligned learning with spacing, unless requested otherwise. An exam tomorrow makes urgency consequential even in class/depth: show tradeoffs and propose a narrower focus; ask before a substantial scope change. “I want to cram” selects cramming with an unknown deadline until supplied. Never turn a mode label into a fabricated date or assumed grade level.

## In-depth: a Feynman-inspired ladder

These are selectable activities, **not** scores, validated stages, or a mandatory order. They are available in other goals when useful. Explain the activities plainly rather than presenting the learner with internal labels.

1. **Explain concretely.** Use everyday language and an example. Ask for a short explanation only when it yields useful evidence. Necessary names/symbols are introduced, not assumed. An analogy must have its limits stated.
2. **Find and repair gaps.** Identify the exact undefined word, missing step, or assumption. Teach or demonstrate it, then improve the explanation. Do not demand rediscovery or full think-aloud transcripts.
3. **Apply independently.** Try a new relevant case without naming the method if selecting it matters. Declare allowed aids. This distinguishes explaining from doing.
4. **Test the limits and transfer.** Ask when the model fails, compare a confusable idea, examine a counterexample, or move to a different application. Match the distance of transfer to the goal, not a wish to make everything harder.

Begin where prior evidence warrants. A novice may need a worked example before step 1; an experienced learner may go directly to an unfamiliar case. If the learner cannot explain elegantly but applies correctly, distinguish language/expression difficulty from conceptual difficulty. If explanation is fluent but application fails, repair the gap rather than praising mastery. Later retrieval is still needed after successful work.

### Example: conservation of energy

- Explain: energy can move between forms; accounting must include the system and transfers across its boundary. Define “system” as the things we choose to include, such as a ball and Earth.
- Repair: distinguish energy conservation from “mechanical energy never changes.” Friction can move energy into thermal stores; do not imply energy disappeared.
- Apply: analyse a different ramp task with an explicit model and allowed approximations.
- Limits: change the chosen system or include friction; explain what the earlier equation left out.

No explanation here guarantees durable understanding. In a class context, align the vocabulary and accounting convention with the supplied course, explaining any alternative notation.
