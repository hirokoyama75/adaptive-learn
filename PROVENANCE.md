# Provenance and reuse

The implementation and teaching instructions in this folder are original work for this specification. No upstream source code or substantial prompt passages were copied. The linked repositories are conceptual references, not runtime dependencies or educational-efficacy evidence.

| Reference, pinned revision | Ideas informing this design | Deliberately not imported |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [amosblomqvist/learn](https://github.com/amosblomqvist/learn/tree/7cfd894) | Relevant prerequisite diagnosis, motivated explanation, making reasoning connections visible. | Exhaustive failure bracketing, mandatory delegation, universal “unconditional truth” doctrine, claims that understanding cannot be forgotten. |
| [amosblomqvist/learnharness](https://github.com/amosblomqvist/learnharness/tree/8f6fd7c) | Independent practice, prewritten solutions/criteria, self-contained tasks, knowledge/evidence separation, joint-premise arguments. | Application infrastructure, graph canvas, storage compatibility, isolated teacher agents, one-attempt locking. |
| [Matt Pocock's teach](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/tree/59db609/skills/productivity/teach) | Observable missions, compact records, terminology consistency, coverage not being learning. | Compulsory HTML output, component libraries, or research for every explanation. |
| [bevibing/tutor-skills](https://github.com/bevibing/tutor-skills/tree/397110c) | Mapping supplied sources, error notes, changed-context revisiting. | Obsidian requirements, four-question multiple-choice batches, accuracy badges as mastery. |

At the original inspection, `learn` had no detected license, LearnHarness was AGPL-3.0-only, and the other two repositories were MIT-licensed. These are historical inspection notes, not a current legal audit. Public visibility is not permission to copy. Any later source or substantial prompt reuse requires license review and applicable notices; no such reuse is necessary here.

The [IES/What Works Clearinghouse guide, *Organizing Instruction and Study to Improve Student Learning* (2007)](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1) supports general practices including spacing, revisiting key content, worked-example/problem alternation, and explanatory questions. Its recommendations have differing evidence levels and scopes; they do not validate this particular skill or every subject/application.

The four in-depth activities are a **Feynman-inspired operationalization**, not an official Feynman scale or an experimentally established sequence. Application and transfer checks intentionally supplement plain-language explanation. There is no guaranteed fastest path, permanent mastery, measured retention probability, or unverified learning-outcome claim.
