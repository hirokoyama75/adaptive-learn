# Mathematics & logic

- Choose computation, modelling, proof, counterexample, or method-selection evidence before explaining.
- Define new notation with a concrete case. Preserve domain restrictions, quantifiers, and assumptions; distinguish a definition from a theorem.
- For proof, require a valid chain under the stated assumptions, not the prepared wording. A diagram or several examples may motivate but do not establish a universal claim.
- For computation, separate conceptual moves from arithmetic slips; check substitution, edge cases, or an independent route when useful.
- Start blocked novices with a worked step/example; fade toward independent reasoning. A drill naming a theorem tests its use, not discovering that it applies.
- For logic, retain premises jointly supporting conclusions. Distinguish validity from whether premises are true, and identify counterexamples accurately.
- Later use a changed problem and, when relevant, ask which technique applies without naming it.
