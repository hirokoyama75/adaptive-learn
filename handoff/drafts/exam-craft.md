# Exam craft
**Draft — not installed.** Handoff bundle only.

Use when the learner knows the content but the format costs marks: running out of time, choosing questions badly, or writing past the mark. Keep the subject playbook for content; this changes how answers are produced. Practise on a real paper rather than reading this list.

## Time per mark

- Divide total minutes by total marks and keep about 10% for reading and checking. A 90-minute, 60-mark paper is roughly 1.3 minutes per mark.
- Check the paper's own instructions; some sections carry more marks than their length suggests.
- Write the budget and the leave-by time on the paper before starting.

## When to skip

- Skip when a question has cost twice its budget with no route forward. Mark it, leave one line of working, move on.
- Do not skip a question that unlocks later parts; partial setup can earn marks without a final answer.
- Return only after the rest is secured, and only for the marks still available.

## Multiple choice

- Answer before reading the options, then compare.
- Eliminate on a stated reason: contradicts a definition, wrong units, wrong direction, answers a different question. If you cannot state the reason, do not eliminate.
- Do not eliminate an option only because it looks unfamiliar or overly precise.
- With two left, prefer the one you can justify in a sentence. Guess only without a penalty and without time to return.

## Open book: index first

- Spend the first two minutes writing page numbers for expected topics. Do not read the book.
- Answer from memory, then check. Reading first spends the time the book was meant to save.
- Confirm what is allowed before the exam, not during it.

## Essay planning

- Allow about a minute per 10 marks: thesis, three to five points, evidence for each.
- Write the plan on the answer sheet; a clear structure can earn credit.
- Leave two lines between paragraphs for a late addition instead of rewriting.

## Partial credit

- Show formula, substitution, and units on separate lines; state the assumption you are using.
- If the answer is uncertain, say what would change it. An unfinished correct method usually outscores a finished guess.

## Oral and viva

- Answer in three moves: direct answer, one reason, one limit. Then stop.
- If you do not know, say what you do know and what you would check; ask for the question once more rather than guessing.
