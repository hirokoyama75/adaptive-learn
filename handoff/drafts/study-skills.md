# Study skills
**Draft — not installed.** Handoff bundle only.

Use when the request is about method rather than content: "teach me to revise", "how do I read this chapter". Pick one method, use it on real material in this session, and keep what works. Retrieval and spacing have the strongest evidence here; the rest are useful defaults, not proven rules. Where evidence is thin, say so and let the learner decide.

## Reading a chapter

- Read headings and the summary first, then write three questions the chapter should answer.
- Read a section, close it, and state the main claim in one sentence. Reopen only to check.
- Stop at a section boundary, not at the end of the chapter.

## Notes

- Write from memory after a section, then fill gaps from the text in a second column.
- Keep one page per topic; if it needs more, split the topic.
- Record the worked example and one condition under which it fails. Both retrieve better than prose.

## A small card set

- Make cards only for what you missed twice, not for everything you met.
- One card, one fact or one step. A list of five items becomes five cards.
- Prune monthly: retire cards answered correctly twice running, and rewrite any card you keep misreading.

## Past papers

- Do the first open-book and untimed, to find what you do not know.
- Do the next timed, with marks visible, and mark it against the scheme before reading the answers.
- Redo only the questions that lost marks; a full re-sit rehearses what you already know.

## Weekly planning

- Choose two or three topics for the week, not the syllabus.
- Start with closed-book recall before new reading.
- Leave one empty block for whatever the week proved harder than expected.

## Memory-heavy loads

- Prefer short, frequent recall to one long session. Block by topic first, mix once each set is reliable.
- Produce the item aloud or in writing, then check; recognition is weaker evidence than production.
- Sleep after a heavy day of new material. It is the part you cannot catch up later.

## What you can now do without me

- At the end of a block, name the task done unaided and the one still assisted.
- Ask the learner to plan the next session alone; review the plan instead of replacing it.
