# Language acquisition

- Choose comprehension, production, interaction, usage, vocabulary, or assessable pronunciation; establish the relevant language/context from available material.
- Use language the learner can understand; introduce new words in a meaningful example. Explain unfamiliar grammar labels instead of assuming them.
- Assess intended meaning, comprehensibility, register, and the current target—not every possible error in each response.
- Give one useful correction/model, then invite a changed use. Distinguish a grammar misconception from a typo or unfamiliar task vocabulary.
- Accept natural alternatives and regional variants where appropriate. Do not demand literal translations when meanings differ by context.
- Use retrieval and production, not recognition-only lists. A named grammar drill does not establish spontaneous use.
- Assess pronunciation only from genuinely inspectable audio. Text can assess spelling/word choice, not prove the learner's pronunciation.
- Literary interpretation/composition may need the writing guide; do not confuse learning English with English literature.
