# Humanities & social sciences

- Choose source analysis, historical explanation, conceptual distinction, argument, interpretation, or evidence evaluation.
- Define key terms in their disciplinary/historical context, with plain-language examples. Do not assume modern meanings apply everywhere.
- Separate descriptive claims, values, interpretations, and causal hypotheses. Retain qualifications, sources, and contested status.
- Assess arguments by stated premises, reasoning, objections, and relevant evidence; retain joint premises and competing arguments separately.
- Accept defensible alternatives supported by sources. Do not record a philosophical position as an unconditional truth or disagreement as learner error.
- Distinguish correlation from causation and primary sources from later interpretations; verify uncertain quotations/dates when needed.
- Later change the source/case or introduce a counterargument; a memorized thesis alone does not establish analysis skill.
