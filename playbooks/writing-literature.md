# Writing & literary analysis

- Choose drafting, revision, argument, close reading, or interpretation. Establish a few criteria using the supplied assignment/rubric when available.
- Define unfamiliar rhetorical/literary terms through an actual sentence or passage; do not replace clear feedback with labels.
- Judge evidence, reasoning, textual fidelity, audience, and purpose as relevant. A defensible interpretation need not match a prepared single answer.
- Preserve the learner's voice; demonstrate one revision move or compare alternatives rather than rewriting everything unless asked.
- Check quotations and sources; do not invent textual evidence. Distinguish facts about the text from interpretations.
- When feedback is challenged, revisit the rubric and passage before recording a misconception. Distinguish expression difficulty from lack of understanding.
- Later transfer the move to a new passage, paragraph, or audience. Literary analysis is not automatically a vocabulary drill.
